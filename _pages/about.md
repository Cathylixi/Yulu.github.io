I am a Doctor of Medicine (MD) student at the [University of Melbourne](https://www.unimelb.edu.au/). I previously completed my Master of Public Health in Epidemiology and Biostatistics at [Boston University School of Public Health](https://www.bu.edu/sph/) and my Bachelor of Science in Human Physiology at [Boston University](https://www.bu.edu/).

**Research Interests.** My interests include clinical epidemiology, biostatistics, kidney disease, gastroenterology, retrospective cohort studies, and quantitative health data analysis.

# Professional Experience

- *Apr 2024 - Nov 2024*, **Intern**, "Stop the Swabs" project, Boston Medical Center.
  - Facilitated IRB submission, conducted retrospective chart reviews, and performed VASES ratings to assess pharyngeal residue in patients at risk for aspiration.
  - Organized data in REDCap, conducted statistical analyses to identify trends, and authored abstracts submitted to the Dysphagia Research Society.
- *Jan 2023 - Present*, **Research Assistant**, CTNNB1 gene mutation study, Boston University School of Medicine.
  - Collected, validated, and analyzed CTNNB1 gene mutation data from 350 patients using databases such as ClinVar, Decipher, and SFARI.
  - Managed data with Google Sheets and Excel to identify associations between mutations and patient demographics.
- *May 2024 - Present*, **Research Assistant**, BU Covid Brain Health study, Boston University School of Medicine.
  - Developed study procedures, created questionnaires, and recruited participants for research on neurocognitive effects of COVID-19 in older adults.
  - Integrated AAAP Staying Sharp data with COVID-19 information to analyze demographic and lifestyle impacts on cognitive function.
- *Jul 2024 - Present*, **Research Assistant**, IBS patient retrospective and prospective study, Boston Medical Center.
  - Provided statistical support for prospective and retrospective IBS studies, managing large datasets and conducting regression analyses using SAS.
  - Developed reports and contributed to publications on sex-based differences and quality-of-life metrics in IBS patients.
